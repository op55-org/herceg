# Changelog

All published packages share one version: `herceg`, `@herceg/react`,
`@herceg/vue`, `@herceg/svelte`, and the Python package `herceg`. Before 1.0,
a minor version may change APIs; each such change is listed here with what
to do about it.

## Unreleased

- Python `hg.Place` accepts panel-scoped side placements for legends and colorbars, matching the engine's validation.
- Chart drag gestures prevent native text selection while preserving static text selection, clicks, and focus.
- Legends name what they list with `include` (`[{ mark, channel }]`, or `[]` for their own `entries` alone), in the `legend` option and `legend()` alike; it replaces `legend()`'s `scales`. A swatch of a point's shape is `{ type: "symbol", symbol }`, replacing `{ type: "circle" }`. Python: `include=`, `hg.Swatch(type="symbol", symbol=...)`.
- The spec loader's validator throws `ChartError`s with codes and paths, and the code `"unsupported"` names a well-formed option a chart does not draw, with what to give instead.
- **Labels following series:** lines and areas take `labels` (`{ at, content, edge, place, style }`, Python `hg.SeriesLabels`): a label per series drawn, at its first or last observation drawn or where it enters or leaves the view (`"visible-start"`, `"visible-end"`), in its color, before its start or after its end, beside the axis with `place: { outside: true }` in room the chart keeps; a band follows the edge `edge` names. Fields are read at the observation a label stands at, as drawn; at the view's edges of a stack split by a series field, where which row stands is not known, fields are refused. Text follows another mark's series with `from: { mark, at, edge }` (Python `from_=hg.MarkAnchor(...)`), drawn where it is listed among the marks. Labels' `content` lists text and parts (`{ field }`, `{ series: true }`, `{ value: true }`, each with a `format`; Python `hg.LabelPart`) for lines', areas', bars', rules', regions', and texts' labels.
- Canvas draws areas' outlines with the joins and ends SVG and PDF draw (round joins, flat ends).
- A line's or area's lone points paint as one dot in every browser (WebKit painted SVG dots twice, and Chromium none of dashed lines in SVG and images), and WebKit paints a highlighted flat line inside its selected runs.
- Value animation moves each band of a series field on its own edges, and a highlight's lit and dimmed copies of a monotone band's or stack's layer whole.
- A mounted chart's failure alert shows its text at once and is styled once its module loads, so a chart's first chunk carries no styling for it.
- **Paint order:** marks on the chart's scales and grid lines take `zIndex` (Python `z_index`; grid lines' as `grid: { zIndex }`, `hg.GridLines(z_index=...)`): within a panel, lower values paint first and ties keep the order given, in SVG, Canvas, PDF, and snapshots alike, and where points meet, the pointer and clicks find the one painted on top.
- **Places of rows:** lines, areas, bars, points, and error bars place rows by coordinates: a field moved `offset` pixels along its axis (`{ field, offset }`), a field on a position scale of the chart's own (`{ field, scale }`, a `scales` entry whose axis `guides` lists on the right or the top), or a field read as a share of the plot or the chart (`{ fraction: { field }, frame }`) or as pixels from its edge (`{ px: { field }, frame }`), each with an offset. Rows placed off their axis count toward none of its domains; rows moved along it are drawn, inspected, and brushed where they are drawn, while y fits rows by their x values (offsets and padding move what is drawn, not what scales fit). Python: `hg.Position`, `hg.Fraction`, `hg.Pixels`.
- **Scale padding:** position scales take `padding`: `"auto"` (the default) leaves room for what marks draw at their values (markers whole, bars' widths) on domains fitted to data and keeps explicit domains and views exact; a share of the span, or `{ px }` at each end; `0` turns it off. Python: `hg.Scale(padding=...)`, `hg.AxisOptions(padding=...)`.
- **Text placement:** text styles' `verticalAlign` (Python `vertical_align`) places blocks of text (annotation labels, region and rule labels, aligned tick labels) above, below, or on their point, padding and box included. Tick labels go inside the plot with `labels: { inside: true }` and stand `gap` pixels from their ticks; flat tick labels whose style sets `align` (x) or `verticalAlign` (y) stand at their ticks, inside or out. The room the chart keeps for labels outside the plots (a rule's beside the y axis) grows with them while the chart's size stays, between panels too.
- **Bars and regions:** bars take `width: { px }`, a fixed width in pixels, and bars and regions `strokeSides` (which sides their outline draws); regions take `stroke` and `strokeWidth`. Python: `width=hg.Pixels(n)`, `stroke_sides=`, and on regions `stroke=` and `stroke_width=`.
- **Web pages:** `herceg/web` carries a chart from a server to a page as one binary payload (`encodePayload`, `decodePayload`, `loadPayload`: a versioned `protocol: 1` envelope around the spec and its buffers, checked before any typed array views it) and mounts page fragments (`observe`, `mount`, `unmount`, `getChart`), which tell the page what the user does with `herceg:ready`, `herceg:error`, `herceg:click`, `herceg:select`, and `herceg:viewport` DOM events of JSON. Python: `herceg.web.assets()` loads the engine once per page from content-hashed files (`manifest()`, `asset_path()`), `herceg.web.fragment(chart, id=, nonce=, data_url=)` writes a chart without the engine and without scripts (strict Content Security Policies need only the nonce), `herceg.web.payload(chart)` makes the bytes a `data_url` route returns, and `selection()`, `inspection()`, and `viewport()` read what pages post back; `herceg.flask.init_app(app)` serves the files and adds the `herceg_assets` and `herceg_chart` template globals (the `flask` extra). The Streamlit component's payload is the same envelope.
- **Native titles in SVG:** the SVG writer can give each bar, point, and observation of a line an invisible pointer target with a `<title>` holding the tooltip's text for its row, over the drawing as it is (`titlesOf`, `renderSvg(scene, { titles })`), for static exports to show tooltips in a viewer; charts over 5,000 such targets refuse it.
- **Colors shared by a field's name:** within a chart, a category field of one name is one mapping: its values take one color and one legend entry in every mark, table, panel, facet, and inset, their places following each table's dictionary in the order marks read them, even where panels have position axes of their own. This replaces sharing by source table: unrelated tables that name a field alike now share its colors. Fields of different names keep apart; a named scale joins them (`encode.color: { field, scale }`), and scales of different names keep fields of one name apart. Value fields still stand for their labels within their table (or the table it is made from). Ordinal scales take `scope: "panel"`, a mapping in each subplot (Python `hg.OrdinalScale(scope="panel")`); facets and marks of every panel refuse it.
- **Identity scales:** a channel through `{ type: "identity" }` (or a named scale of that type; Python `hg.IdentityScale`) draws a field's values as they are: colors, shapes, and dashes from category fields, sizes (areas in square pixels for points), widths, and opacities from number fields, each checked against the channel drawing it. Identity colors stand for nothing: they split no series, make no legend entries, and points keep each color exact however many there are (points held 254 colors before). A line whose series a named identity scale would split is refused; give `series`.
- **Points' channels:** points read category fields, named scales, and conditions for `color` (or `fill`), `size`, `fillOpacity` (or `encode.opacity`), and `symbol` alike, and a symbol field's shapes through the `"symbol"` scale of its name, so `scales: { symbol: { map } }` picks them; sizes and opacities of category fields need a named scale. A value a scale leaves unmapped draws muted, unshaped (not drawn), at the mark's size, or opaque. Setting one channel both flat and in `encode` is refused, and `encode.stroke` and `encode.strokeWidth` take values (one outline per mark). A malformed constant or condition branch is refused as the mark is made.
- **Style runs:** a line's or area's look changes along a series where `encode` reads rows (a field other than the series', a condition, an identity scale), without splitting it: a piece of line takes the look of the observation it arrives at, and a step's flat run the look of the value it holds, so a forecast flagged on its own rows draws dashed from the last observation on. Each run draws its own stretch of the series' path, in path order: observations sharing a position are joined in their arriving looks, tangents are shared, dashes carry on across boundaries their pattern stays over, the join where two runs meet is painted once where the path near it is two straight segments (half by each, so translucent looks do not darken seams; in SVG, canvas, and PDF), and elsewhere (dense or curved paths, sharp turns) the later run paints over the earlier, level of detail keeps every run, markers take their own rows' looks, selections highlight runs as drawn (selected rows standing alone as dots in their own looks, painted in row order over the dimmed series as translucent points are), and the legend keeps one entry per series. Positions that do not ascend, and layers of a stack, refuse looks that change. This replaces the `series-varies` error for `encode`; `style.by` fields still must be constant within a series. A line colored by a field other than its series' stands for that field's value, in the legend and as hidden, while every one of its rows has it, and for itself where the value changes along it.
- **Legend composition:** a legend's `include` lists a scale of categories by name (`{ scale }`), a mark's scale (`{ mark, channel }`), or a mark whole (`{ mark }`, Python `hg.MarkRef`), and its own `entries` come after what it includes; `include: []` keeps them alone. Rule, region, and text annotations with `legend: true` are listed by their labels (one entry, or one per value of a label's field) in the look they draw, hiding and showing the annotation or its rows of that value. Entries target a mark whole, a field's values (a series field's series, or the rows of points, unstacked bars, and annotations holding them), or a scale's values in every mark reading it (`hg.LegendTarget`); they name values, so series and rows that come later follow them. Panels' legends list their panel's annotations too. Listed legends on one side stack in the order `guides` gives them, the first nearest the plots. Legend entries show what they name as drawn, in the chart and in each panel: hidden where all of it is, whatever hides it (its mark hidden whole, predicates over all of its rows, a panel's own settings), mixed where some of it is, and with no say where nothing drawn has it; showing one shows all of it, letting go of everything hiding any of it: a mark's whole key, what its series stand for, a predicate over its rows, in the chart or its panel (`LegendItem.shows`). The default legend shows marks hidden whole and rows hidden by value too. A legend's entries need keys of their own (`invalid-option`), and colorbars name a mark's color ramp, maps' included, rather than a scale by name (`invalid-option`); a colorbar over a map of no values waits for some.
- **Images for documents:** `renderToImage(chartOrOptions, settings)` from the new `herceg/image` writes PNG, JPEG, and WebP in browsers, and Python's `chart.to_image(format=, size=, unit=, dpi=, quality=, background=, fonts=)` without one (resvg and Pillow, which joins the `export` extra). `size` and `unit` (`"px"`, `"in"`, `"cm"`, `"mm"`, `"pt"`) lay the chart out and `dpi` (96 by default) sets the pixels only, recorded in the file (PNG `pHYs`, JPEG JFIF density, WebP EXIF); `quality` is for JPEG and WebP; JPEG is drawn on `background`, else the chart's background, else white (black under a transparent chart's light text, which white would hide); limits are refused before anything is allocated; a mounted chart is drawn at its own size and never resized. `chart.toPNG({ dpi })` and Python's `to_png(dpi=)` replace `scale`. `save()` chooses PNG, JPEG (`.jpg`, `.jpeg`), WebP, SVG, PDF, or HTML by the extension, and refuses a `format` that contradicts it and options of other formats.
- **Export settings apart from the chart:** `renderToSVG(chartOrOptions, settings)` and `specToSVG(spec, buffers, settings)` take how to write the file as their last argument: `sizing`, `preserveAspectRatio`, `strokeScaling`, `fonts`, `measureText`, and `idPrefix` move there from the chart options, and `size` and `unit` (an SVG in inches or millimeters says so in its width and height) and `tooltip: "title"` (native `<title>` tooltips on bars, points, and line observations, up to 5,000; refused for images and PDF) are new. Python: `to_svg(size=, unit=, tooltip=)`. `renderToSVG` also takes a mounted chart, as it is. `StaticOptions` (chart options with `width` and `height`) replaces `PdfOptions`.
- **Express:** each plot takes `z_index`, and those drawn by lines, areas, bars, or points `encode` (and lines and bars `labels`), forwarded to the mark that draws the result. They read the data's columns, which are carried into the tables the plot computes and refused where a summary would need one value of a column that varies within its group, with what to do instead; columns the plot computes take names apart from the data's. Its other marks for the same series take that mark's color binding (a column or the series through a scale), and are gray beside a color of its own. An `x_axis` or `y_axis` given to an express function keeps the column's name as its title unless it sets one.

The first public release. What it contains:

**Engine (`herceg`)**
- **Data and specs:**
  - columnar tables of typed arrays, with appends, rolling windows (which keep category dictionaries bounded, counting their renumberings in `categories(field).revision`), replacements, and row views;
  - categories, times, derived tables, and portable expressions;
  - a serializable chart spec (`herceg/spec`) that every surface shares.
- **Marks:**
  - line, area, bar, point, heatmap, histogram, box plot, letter-value (boxen) plot, violin, error bar, candlestick, rug, and dendrogram;
  - rule, region, and text annotations;
  - pie, treemap, sunburst, gauge, funnel, radar, Sankey, network, maps (shapes and points), and 3D points, lines, and surfaces;
  - custom marks;
  - matplotlib dash strings (`"-"`, `"--"`, `":"`, `"-."`) on lines, rules, and custom shape styles, scaled by stroke width.
- **Composition:** marks compose in one chart, in facets, and in subplots, with shared or independent scales, right axes, and range navigators; 3D layers share CPU-rendered subplots with independent cameras and controls (3D facets are refused).
- **Layouts of panels (`herceg/layout`):** subplots go where a layout puts them: `columns(widths, children)`, `rows(heights, children)`, `grid({ rows, columns, gap, id }, cells)` with `cell({ row, column, rowSpan, columnSpan }, content)`, and `mosaic(rows of ids)`, nested as deep as needed, given to `panels(list, layout)` or `panels(layout)` with each panel's settings where it goes (`panel({ id?, title, x, y, ... })`; ids left out are `"panel-1"`, `"panel-2"`, ... by declaration order). Tracks are weights, `{ weight, min }`, or `{ px }`, and size the panels' plots, whose edges line up across tracks; a layout that does not fit is refused with the room it needs. Scopes take `{ layout, by }` and `{ panels }` beside `"chart"`, `"panel"`, `"row"`, and `"column"`, and axes take `repeat: "outer" | "all"`. In specs, the chart's `layout`.
- **Panels have scales of their own by default:** in subplots, x and y are each panel's unless a scope shares them (x is the chart's with a range navigator); facets share them.
- **Insets:** `inset(ref, { place, width, height, indicator })` draws a panel over another's plot, with scales of its own (the chart's `"chart"` scope leaves insets out), its axes in its box, and optionally an indicator of its ranges on the parent joined to it; the pointer, wheel, and brush act in the topmost panel, crosshairs and data stop at insets, and every renderer, export, and server snapshot draws them.
- **Panels' legends:** `legend({ panel })` lists what one panel draws and hides and shows it there only; the chart's legend lists what panels' legends leave out (`legend: true` keeps everything) and shows an entry hidden in some panels as mixed. `setVisibility(keys, visible, { panel })`, `getHiddenKeys({ panel })`, and the `visibility` event's `panels` carry visibility per panel.
- **Scales:** `x`, `y`, and `yRight` are the scales "x", "y", and "yRight" with their axes. Their `scope` (`"chart"`, `"panel"`, `"row"`, or `"column"`) says which facet panels or subplots share one instance; it replaces the chart's `resolve` option (Python: `hg.AxisOptions(scope=...)` replaces `resolve_x`, `resolve_y`, and `resolve_y_right`). A scale's type comes from the marks of the panels sharing it, so one subplot can show categories along an axis while another shows numbers.
- **Scales and guides:** `scales` (`{ x, y, yRight }`, scale settings over the short forms') and `guides` (`axis(...)`, `legend(...)`, `colorbar(...)`) add axes, legends, and colorbars beyond the automatic ones. Ticks are a count, `{ every }` (calendar steps such as `"6 hours"` or `"month"` in the chart's time zone, or numbers from an anchor), `{ values }`, or a function; formats are Intl options, `{ number }` d3-like patterns, `{ time }` strftime patterns, or a function; specs refuse functions at their path. Axes take tick layers (minor ticks, day boundaries in a second row) with tick direction and length, label rotation candidates, and collisions (`"hide"`, `"stagger"`, `"wrap"`, `"show"`), and go on the top (x) or right (y). Legends take `columns` or `rows`, `order`, a `title`, custom `entries`, `merge: "semantic"` (one entry per series value across marks and panels), and `swatches: "mark"`; marks take `legend: false`. Colorbars draw a continuous color scale along its domain, vertical or horizontal, with ticks, a title, and a format, or a contour's bands as steps, and are described with their ranges. Legends and colorbars naming a mark show it whatever its `legend` setting; a mark's `"color"` is its series' colors where its color splits them, and a reference to a scale the mark's options do not give it is an error naming those it has, whatever its rows hold (a scale of no values yet is shown once it has some). The guide compiler loads only with charts that use these. Python: `hg.Scale`, `hg.ScaleRef`, `hg.Ticks`, `hg.Format`, `hg.Tick`, `hg.TickLabels`, `hg.TickLayer`, `hg.axis`, `hg.legend`, `hg.colorbar`, `hg.LegendEntry`, `hg.Swatch`, and `legend=False` on marks; `hg.AxisOptions` and `hg.LegendOptions` are the short forms' settings.
- **Styles and themes:** a theme is tokens (`background`, `text`, `muted`, `grid`, `axis`, `palette`, `sequential`, `diverging`, `rise`, `fall`) and role styles (`text`, `title`, `panelTitle`, `axisTitle`, `tickLabel`, `legendLabel`, `legendTitle`, `plot`, `grid`, `axisLine`, `tick`, and the marks' `line`, `bar`, and `tooltip` defaults) over the built-in theme it `extends`: `theme: { extends, tokens, style }`, which replaces the theme's top-level keys (`font`, `fontSize`, `titleSize`, `gridDash`, `lineWidth`, `barRadius`, `tooltipBackground`, `tooltipBorder` become `style` roles). Axes, the legend, the title, and subplots take a `style` of their roles, cascading over the theme's. Text styles set font, size, weight, slant, color, opacity, line height, alignment, halo, a background box, and padding (the box goes where its text would go without one, and the text moves in by the padding); line styles set stroke, width, dash, and opacity; the plot style a background and border. Colors and fonts may be CSS variables in themes, styles, and every color option (marks' paints, scale maps, channel values and conditions, looks by value, markers, ramps, custom marks): charts in a page read them from it, so SVG, Canvas, the GPU painter, tooltips, and hover dots draw the same color, and read them again when the page switches themes; static output takes their fallbacks, refuses a variable without one at the option that set it, and holds no `var()`. Python: `hg.Theme`, `hg.Tokens`, `hg.Style`, `hg.TextStyle`, `hg.StrokeStyle`, `hg.PlotStyle`, `hg.LineStyle`, `hg.BarStyle`, `hg.TooltipStyle`, `hg.TextBox`.
- **Layout:** `padding` (pixels, or per side; `0` lets a chart fill its size; left out, spacing is automatic); titles as a string or `{ text, place, style }`, at the top or bottom, aligned by their style; `"\n"` breaks every label into lines (titles, annotations, mark labels, legends and their keys, axis, colorbar, subplot, and facet titles, category tick labels, tooltips), measured and placed as a block in every renderer and export, while names layout marks fit to one line (slices, nodes, stages, spokes) and number and time labels on the automatic axes are written on one; subplot titles as a string or `{ text, style }`; legends as `true`, `false`, or `{ place, style }`, placed on any side (`align`, `offset`) or inside a plot's corner or edge (`inset`, `scope`, `panel`). Python: `hg.Title`, `hg.LegendOptions`, `hg.Place`, `padding=`.
- **Marks:** `clip` (`"plot"`, `"chart"`, or `false`) on marks on the chart's scales; `halo` (`"auto"`, `false`, or `{ color, width?, opacity? }`) on `text`, `region`, `bar`, `funnel`, `radar`, `sankey`, `graph`, and `rangeNavigator`, replacing its boolean form; `"auto"` is the color of the surface under the text, whatever the text's own. Python: `clip=`, and `halo=` taking `"auto"`, `False`, or `hg.Halo`.
- **Fonts (`herceg/fonts`):** `prepareFonts(sources)` gives a chart and every export the same static OpenType faces (`fonts` on chart options, `renderToSVG`, `specToSVG`, `renderToPDF`, and `specToPDF`): live charts measure with them and register them with the page, SVG and PNG exports measure with them, PDF embeds subsets, and text in them is drawn unkerned, as measured. Faces with CFF outlines (most `.otf` files) measure and draw in the browser, SVG, and PNG; PDF embeds TrueType (`glyf`) outlines only and refuses them, saying so. Variable fonts are refused. SVG, PNG, and PDF exports, a chart's `toSVG()` and `toPNG()` among them, set text glyph after glyph along the theme's font list and refuse alike text no face in it covers, wherever it is drawn, naming the code point and the fix, where PNG drew boxes; live charts and snapshot markup, which the page draws, fall back to its fonts. `fallback` and `missingGlyph` are PDF export settings. It replaces `pdfFonts` from `herceg/pdf` (`FontSource`, `FontOptions`, and `Fonts` replace `PdfFontSource`, `PdfFontOptions`, and `PdfFonts`).
- **Positions:** the portable `Coordinate` (a data value on a named scale, a field, a fraction of the plot or chart, or pixels from its edge, with a pixel offset) and `Position` (one per axis) types; annotations' number-or-field positions are typed `AnnotationValue`.
- **Encodings:** `color` is a mark's primary paint (a line's stroke, a bar's, area's, or point's fill): a category field through the chart's `"color"` scale, or a CSS color; `stroke` and `fill` win over it. `encode` takes channels by field and named scale, constant, or condition (`{ condition: { when, then, otherwise } }` over portable expressions) for color, fill, stroke, dash, strokeWidth, and opacity on lines, areas, and bars. Scales of categories (`scales: { color: { type: "ordinal", domain, map, unknown } }`) give a value one output in every mark and panel, before facets and hidden series, in the domain's or the dictionary's order, and keep it while data streams. Points' category colors go through the color scale, in the field's dictionary order. `style.by` maps series (or bars' rows) to looks by a field's value or by series name; lines take `markers` and `style.marker`, and their looks show in legends with `swatches: "mark"`; `opacity` on lines, areas, and bars, and `strokeOpacity` on those and rules. Python: `hg.Channel`, `hg.Encodings`, `hg.when`, `hg.OrdinalScale(...)`, `hg.MarkStyle`, `hg.StyleMap`, `hg.SeriesStyle`, `hg.MarkerStyle`.
- **Color implies series:** a category `color` field splits lines, areas (range areas too), bars, and points (along axes, on maps, and in 3D) into series, as `series` does, unless the mark gives `series` (`series: false` for none, which colors bars and points along axes row by row; map and 3D points refuse it) or its own stroke or fill; histograms, box plots, boxen plots, violins, error bars, and rugs take `color` too, a category field naming their series or a color. A line or area whose color field changes within a series draws its colors in runs along it (see style runs). Marks without `color` refuse it, naming what to give instead. Python: the same in `hg.*` marks (`series=False`), and `herceg.express` helpers take `color=` as their series column, before statistics.
- **Color identity:** a value has one color and one legend entry in every mark and panel, its palette slot its place in the field's dictionaries (a Categorical's unused categories keep theirs) whether color splits series or not, layout marks' items included (pie slices, top-level tree branches, radar series, map regions and points, network node groups, 3D points and lines); value fields stand for their labels within their table, following tables made from one table back to it (derived tables, row views, and spec tables naming their `origin`); named scales join values on purpose (`encode.color: { field, scale }`, or `{ series: true, scale }` for series' names). Colors stay while data is appended, dropped, replaced, and hidden.
- **Legends by identity:** a legend lists one entry per value, which toggles every series standing for it, with each mark's look under `swatches: "mark"`. This replaces the legend's `merge: "semantic"` and marks' `legendGroup` (Python `merge="semantic"` and `legend_group`), which are removed: drop them; marks of one field share entries without them.
- **Errors you can see:** `ChartError` (`code`, `phase`, `path`, `mark`, `panel`; `errorInfo` as JSON) for what a chart refuses, in one shape for every surface. A chart that cannot draw shows a box of its size saying "Chart could not be rendered" and why, or over a last frame still right, "Chart update failed; showing previous data.", and reports it once to `onError`, `chart.on("error")`, and `chart.ready`; `errorMode: "throw"` throws instead. Exports (`toSVG`, `toPNG`) throw what keeps a frame from drawing.
- **Line breaks and conditional bands:** `continuity: { defined, maxGap, breakBy }` on lines and areas decides which observations connect before they are reduced, so reduced lines never bridge a break; `where` leaves rows out as gaps, ending runs where the condition changes (interpolated on linear curves, exact on step curves), so conditional bands meet their lines, in time axis breaks leave and on log scales too. Value domains, text summaries, and tooltips leave those rows out; positions' domains keep every row. Python: `hg.Continuity`, `hg.Duration` (or `timedelta`), `break_by=hg.col(...)` for a field, `where=`.
- **Bars:** series stand side by side at each position, as before, unless `dodge: false` overlaps them or `stack` piles them; `series` partitions and `dodge` places on every mark, with a default of its own for bars (points, lines, areas, and error bars dodge only when asked). A category `color` field splits bars into series (see Color implies series); with `series: false`, each bar takes its row's color and keeps its whole band. Bars take `stroke`, `strokeWidth`, `strokeOpacity`, `dash`, and `opacity`; colored by a field other than their series', they list its values in a key. Value labels are `true` or a label object `{ field, format, style, minLength, place }`, replacing Intl options (now `format`), inside the bars with `place: { outside: false, attachment }`; labels inside bars are white wherever white reaches 3:1 contrast, else black. Python: `hg.BarLabels`.
- **Annotations:** places along an axis are values, fields, or coordinates (`{ data }`, `{ field }`, `{ fraction }`, `{ px }`, each with an `offset`); text takes `at: { x, y }`; rules take a `span` and `strokeOpacity`. `label` is text or a label object `{ text | field, format, style, place }`, replacing `textField`, and for text `anchor`, `fill`, `dx`, and `dy` (use `label: { field }`, `style: { align, color }`, and coordinate offsets). A rule's label can go outside the plot beside the y axis, in room the chart keeps for it (`place: { attachment: "start", outside: true }`). Annotations no longer widen the axes' domains unless they say `contributeDomain: true`, so they show where the data is: thresholds meant to stay in view say so, or the chart sets a domain. Python: `hg.Label`, `hg.LabelPlace`, `hg.Position`, `hg.Fraction`, `hg.Pixels`, `hg.DataPosition`, `contribute_domain=`; `text_field`, `anchor`, `fill`, `dx`, and `dy` are gone.
- **Stacks:** an area's `stack` takes `{ offset, order, gaps }`, replacing `stackOrder` and `stackGaps` (Python: `hg.Stack`, replacing `stack_order` and `stack_gaps`).
- **Symbols:** points and markers take `"x"`, a cross turned 45 degrees; `"cross"` stays a plus.
- **Rendering:** SVG or Canvas chosen per chart, WebGL for large point clouds and standalone 3D charts, and level-of-detail reduction for large lines and areas.
- **Interaction:**
  - tooltips with configurable fields;
  - keyboard reading;
  - zoom and pan;
  - brushes and lassos;
  - row selections shared between charts, which summaries (histograms, box plots, violins, heatmaps, pies, funnels, trees, Sankey diagrams) show as each item's selected part, in exports too;
  - synced cursors and views;
  - drilldown, map and network navigation, and node dragging;
  - per-panel 3D cameras through `getCamera({ panel })`, `setCamera(camera, { panel })`, and scoped camera events.
- **Gestures and bindings:** `interaction.bindings` binds gestures (the wheel by axis and keys held, or only while the chart has the focus; drags by mouse button and kind of pointer; pinches; keys; double clicks) to actions (`pan`, `zoom`, `brush`, `reset`, `cursor`, `orbit`, `moveItem`, `pageScroll`), tried in order before the presets. `pan`, `zoom`, `brush`, `rotate`, and `drag` are presets of bindings, and maps, networks, trees, and 3D charts match the same bindings as axis charts. Duplicate gestures, the keyboard cursor's keys without Ctrl, Alt, or Meta, and brushes that zoom while selecting rows are refused. Python: `bindings=[hg.Binding(gesture, action)]` with `hg.WheelGesture`, `hg.DragGesture`, `hg.PinchGesture`, `hg.KeyGesture`, `hg.DoubleClickGesture`, and `hg.PanAction`, `hg.ZoomAction`, `hg.BrushAction`, `hg.ResetAction`, `hg.CursorAction`, `hg.OrbitAction`, `hg.MoveItemAction`, `hg.PageScrollAction`.
- **Behavior changes in interaction:**
  - the vertical wheel scrolls the page over every chart; it zooms only with Ctrl or ⌘ held (as trackpad pinches send it), in axis charts, maps, networks, and 3D charts alike, and a touch pinch zooms charts that zoom;
  - the horizontal wheel, and Shift with the wheel, pan x in charts that pan or zoom along x;
  - `interaction.pan` (`true`, `"x"`, `"y"`, `"xy"`) pans by dragging without zooming; `zoom` still pans by dragging, unless `pan: false`; layout-only charts (pies, treemaps, gauges, ...) refuse `pan`;
  - `+` (or `=`) and `-` zoom by 1.25 about the plot's middle and `0` resets in axis charts that zoom, as in maps and networks;
  - double-click resets in charts that pan, as in charts that zoom;
  - Escape during a drag ends it: a brush drawn so far is dropped, and a pan goes back to where it started;
  - touch: charts that brush or pan along one axis leave pinches to the page (`touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` rather than `pan-y`), and charts that zoom take them;
  - the code that answers input (tooltips, the pointer, the wheel, keys, the keyboard cursor, crosshairs) loads right after the chart's first frame, or at its first input; input that arrives earlier is kept and handled once it loads, and `chart.loading` is pending until then, so tests that check what input does right after creating a chart await it;
  - crosshair labels on the axes (`tooltip.axisLabels`) are HTML boxes placed by CSS, and moving the cursor measures no text;
  - the cursor shows across a chart's panels that share an x scale (facets, subplots): where the pointer or keyboard is in one, the others show a crosshair and their points at the same x (`sync: { panels: false }` turns it off);
  - the keyboard's cursor is shared with synced charts, as the pointer's is;
  - synced selections carry categories by name rather than by index, so charts whose categories differ or come in another order share them; a selection of categories arrives as the range from the first to the last of them in the receiving chart's order.
- **View limits:** `interaction.limits` keeps each scale's view inside an `extent` (`{ x: { extent: [from, null] } }`), however it is asked for (the pointer, keys, `setViewport`, brushes, synced charts; a view fitted to the data shows the data), and with `edge` reports an `edge` event (`scale`, `side`, `domain`, `bound`, `panels`, `cause`) when the view comes near an end (the extent's, or on a shared x scale, the data's), again only after it moved away by `hysteresis` or the end moved, so applications load history as the reader pans back while the view holds. Events say what moved the view (`ViewCause`). Adapters: `onEdge`, `@edge`, `onedge`. Python: `limits={"x": hg.ViewLimits(extent=..., edge=True)}`, `hg.Edge`, and `widget.on_edge` with `hg.ViewEdge`.
- **Sync channels:** `sync` takes `{ cursor, viewport, selection }`, each a group's name or `{ group, scale }`, so charts share the cursor alone, or follow another chart's x along their y; a group's name alone shares all three along x, as before. Values travel as data, and charts never share again what they receive. The cursor's channel takes `snap` (`"nearest"`, the default; `"previous"`, each series' last point at or before the shared value, for readings at other frequencies; `"next"`) and `tooltip: true` (the receiving chart's own tooltip at the cursor, one to a chart). `sync.panels` shares the cursor across the chart's own panels (`true`, `false`, or `{ snap, tooltip }`; on by default for panels sharing x). `chart.showCursor({ x }, { snap, tooltip })` and `chart.hideCursor()` show and hide the cursor as a synced chart's (shared unless `{ broadcast: false }`). Python: `sync=hg.Sync(cursor=..., viewport=..., selection=..., panels=...)` with `hg.SyncChannel(group, scale=, snap=, tooltip=)`, and `widget.show_cursor(x=..., snap=..., tooltip=...)` and `widget.hide_cursor()`.
- **Server rendering:** `renderToSnapshot(options, { width, height, idPrefix, fonts, locale, timeZone })` renders a chart without a DOM, as the browser runtime builds it, with deterministic ids and what hydrating needs, and `hydrateChart(host, options, snapshot)` takes that markup over without drawing it again (drawing only what differs, keeping the markup while code it waits for loads, and refusing markup the snapshot does not describe). `specToSnapshot` does so for specs. React, Vue, and Svelte `<Chart>` take `initial={hydration(snapshot)}` and hydrate without a mismatch or flash; `hydration` brings the adoption code, which other pages do not carry. Python: `chart.to_snapshot(id_prefix=...)`.
- **Accessibility:** generated descriptions, keyboard navigation, and accessible legends; tested with axe-core in Chrome, Firefox, and WebKit.
- **Export:** static SVG, PNG, and tagged multi-page PDF, without a DOM where possible; fluid SVG with `sizing: "fluid"`, `preserveAspectRatio`, and `strokeScaling: "fixed"` (`renderToSVG`, `specToSVG`, `chart.toSVG(sizing)`; Python `to_svg(sizing=..., preserve_aspect_ratio=..., stroke_scaling=...)`).
- **Analytical grid (`herceg/grid`):** sorting, filtering, grouping, pivots, remote data, and row selection.

**Framework adapters:** `@herceg/react`, `@herceg/vue`, and `@herceg/svelte`. Charts are written as components, while data updates bypass the framework. Errors show in the chart's place, reach `onError` (`@error`, `onerror`), and with `errorMode="throw"` are thrown in render for error boundaries. Each adapter's `/layout` has `Columns`, `Rows`, `Grid`, `Cell`, `Panel`, and `Inset` components, whose marks take the panel they are in, and `/guides` has `Legend`, a panel's own inside a `Panel`.

**Python (`herceg`)**
- `hg.*` charts from pandas, Polars, PyArrow, and NumPy data, sent as binary columns;
- output to notebooks, HTML, SVG, PNG, and PDF;
- layouts of subplots built cell by cell: `chart.columns([2, 1])`, `rows`, `grid(rows=, columns=)` with `cell(row=, column=, row_span=, column_span=)`, and `mosaic([...])` give cells whose `panel(id=None, weight=, title=, ...)` makes a panel taking mark methods (`latency.line(...)`), insets (`panel.inset(...)`), and a legend of its own (`panel.legend(...)`); `layout=hg.Grid(columns=2)` places registered panels; tracks take `hg.Weight(n, min=)` and `hg.Pixels(n)`;
- live notebook widgets and a Streamlit component, with camera state per 3D panel, and each panel's own view and selection where panels have x scales of their own (`widget.panel_viewports`, `on_viewport(..., panel=)`, `hg.Selection(panels=...)`; facet panels with x scales of their own are refused in widgets);
- `herceg.express`: Seaborn-style statistical charts with recompute on new data; the functions drawing one plot take `panel=`, a panel of another chart, which they return (`hx.statistics(chart, panel=...)`);
- helpful unknown-option errors for matplotlib names, restricted to the callable's supported options;
- `hg.ChartError`, a `ValueError` with the engine's code and path, raised as marks are added (unknown columns, listing those there are; column kinds; inferred series with several value columns), as charts are output (scale references), and from the engine; HTML, notebooks, widgets, and Streamlit show a chart that cannot draw in its place.
