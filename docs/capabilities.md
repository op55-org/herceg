[test/contracts/](../test/contracts/) and a complete passing run of every test
suite (see [Contracts](contributing.md#contracts)); do not edit.

the package publishes the marks' matrix, without them, as `herceg/capabilities.json`.
[test/contracts/api.json](../test/contracts/api.json) lists where option names differ
between TypeScript, Python, and the adapters, and why.

- ✓ works, proved by the test file listed with the capability.
- ○ supported, but not yet proved: declared to work, with no test proving it yet.
- ✗1 refused: asking for it fails with note 1's message, which names an alternative tested to work.
- ·2 not applicable, for note 2's reason.

## Placement

- **Named panel**: The row's mark drawn in one of two panels of one size draws what it draws in the other; the line beside it draws as alone; no other panel draws anything. Proved in [placement.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/contracts/placement.test.ts).
- **Facets**: Split by the chart's facet field into panels N and S, each panel draws what the mark draws over that group's rows alone. Proved in [placement.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/contracts/placement.test.ts).
- **Facets in a layout cell**: A layout cell splits the mark's rows into facet panels inside it.
- **Nested layout**: In columns of rows of four panels of one size, the mark draws in its panel what it draws in another, at its place in the nesting; the others draw nothing of it. Proved in [placement.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/contracts/placement.test.ts).
- **Spanning cell**: In a cell spanning both columns above two panels, the mark draws as in the top panel of rows of a panel and a grid of two: across both columns. Proved in [placement.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/contracts/placement.test.ts).
- **Inset**: In one of two insets of one size over a panel, the mark draws what it draws in the other; the panel draws its line as without it. Proved in [placement.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/contracts/placement.test.ts).

## Events

- **Click event**: A click on an item reports it as the click event's target: its mark's index and id, and its key, the same at the next click; where the mark reads a table, with the rows it stands for (those a click on it selects, else its own row). (emits; events `click`) Proved in [events.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/browser/contracts/events.test.ts).
- **Pin**: With tooltip.pin "click", a click on an item the tooltip reads pins the tooltip there: one pin event, at the item, or at its value along the inspection's axis; a second click there lets it go, reported too. (emits; events `pin`; options `tooltip.pin`) Proved in [events.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/browser/contracts/events.test.ts).
- **Drill**: With interaction.zoom, a click on a group drills into it: one drill event naming the group's key and path, which getDrillRoot then reads; Backspace goes back out to the whole tree, reported too. (emits; events `drill`; options `interaction.zoom`) Proved in [events.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/browser/contracts/events.test.ts).
- **Item move**: A network's node (interaction.drag) or a rule of a value (drag) moves by a drag: itemMove reports its key and where it is dropped (a node's place, which it keeps; a rule's value along its axis, after previews as it moves). (emits; events `itemMove`; options `interaction.drag`, `marks[].drag`) Proved in [events.test.ts](../packages/herceg/test/browser/contracts/events.test.ts).

## Express recipes

Each recipe is all the marks it draws, proved together in Python.

- **Statistics**: hx.statistics(chart) gives what the recipe computed (estimates, intervals, densities, fits, bins), equal to the fixture's values computed apart, and with panel= those of the plot drawn in that panel.
- **Recompute**: hx.recompute(chart, data) makes the recipe again from new data: what it draws and its statistics follow the new rows, together (an estimate with its band), and marks added after it read the new table.

## GPU paths

Where a GPU draws marks (WebGL2), which no renderer option asks for: the chart hands geometry past a size to the GPU painter, and draws it on the CPU where WebGL2 is unavailable. Each path's proofs show the painter holding the mark's data and the canvas showing it, and the fallback drawing it without WebGL2.
